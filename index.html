How this record is made

The CHPC page draws recent status as forty-eight equal-width blocks labelled 4h ago on the left and now on the right. There is no API behind it and no timestamp in the markup, so the tempting reading is forty-eight blocks over four hours, five minutes each.

That reading does not survive measurement. Sampling the live bar and cross-correlating consecutive reads shows how far the pattern scrolled left. Every fit below was exact, so the shifts are not in doubt:

Interval (UTC)ElapsedBlockss / block
19:21:10 → 19:27:29379 s1379
19:27:29 → 19:33:31362 s3121
19:33:31 → 19:36:14163 s1163
19:21:10 → 19:36:14904 s5181

Two things follow. The mean rate is about three minutes per block, so the bar spans roughly 2.4 hours, not the four it claims. More importantly the spacing is not uniform — 379 seconds for one block, then 121 seconds each for three. The bar behaves like the last forty-eight checks at an irregular cadence, not like a fixed time axis. Absolute times cannot be recovered from the strokes alone.

So the poller does not try. lengau_status.py runs on a GitHub-hosted runner every ten minutes and records the status against its own clock — that interval, and nothing else, is the resolution of the trace above. The faded blocks are the page's own bar decoded onto its nominal axis, written only where the poller missed a stretch and something beats nothing. The hatched stretches are the honest admission that nothing was watching.
